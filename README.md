# From Query to Answer: Children’s Search Behaviour in the Classroom

## Abstract
Children turn to search engines (SE) from an early age, yet familiarity with these systems does not necessarily translate into expertise. Further, much of the research on children search predates recent advances in SE and children's increased digital exposure, so it is unclear whether prior findings reflect current practices. We investigate how primary school children use SE in the classroom context. We analyse search logs from grade 3 and 4 students (ages 7–10) answering curriculum-related questions, identifying common search practices throughout the different stages of the information seeking process (ISP). Our results show that, despite deviating from the search behaviours anticipated by existing ISP models, children can still find the information they need, highlighting potential limitations of these models in capturing children's distinct search practices. Our nuanced analysis also evinces the challenges children encounter across different ISP stages which may impede their information access, revealing possible areas of intervention for interactive information retrieval, including child-oriented retrieval strategies to support young users' development into proficient online searchers.
