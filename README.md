# Search Hurdles: An empirical exploration of ineffective child-system interactions during information seeking

## Abstract
Children's interactions with online information access systems (IAS), like Google and Bing, from an early age not only expose them to content that shapes their perception of the world but also lay the foundations for their expectations and subsequent behaviours when seeking information online. While such early exposure suggests a degree of familiarity with mainstream IAS, it remains unclear how effectively young users navigate and benefit from modern search environments. In this work, we investigate the extent to which children's common search behaviours may hinder them from acquiring the information they need. For this, we empirically examine children's search behaviours using search logs collected from primary-grade students interacting with a web search interface to answer curriculum-related questions. We analyse their behaviours across three phases of the search process—query formulation, search results navigation, and information distillation—to identify common behaviours that yield poor outcomes at each phase. With our findings, we aim to support the design of child-tailored information retrieval strategies that can guide young users into becoming competent online searchers.
